# A 1500 to 1800 design reference for Colonial Campaign

The reference pool spans European settlements in the Americas, with emphasis on Atlantic North America and the Caribbean and a separate mining example from the Andes. Availability varied sharply by century, region, and settlement size. The numbers are proposed game values. The classic comparison uses the bundled Colonization manual and Player Aid Cards. The campaign comparison refers to v006.

## Ships and inland transport

Move points are squares per turn, holds are 100 goods or one passenger each, and strength uses roughly the classic scale. Draft is a proposed access rule. The period bands suggest unlock windows for this game and are approximate.

| Vessel and period role | Moves / holds / strength | Classic and v006 comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Caravel, early exploration and light service | 4 / 2 / 2, shallow | Classic and v006 starter |
| Carrack or nao, early ocean transport | 3 / 5 / 4, deep | Classic compresses this niche into larger merchants |
| Galleon, long-distance merchant or armed transport | 5 / 6 / 10, deep | Classic gives 6 moves. v006 sells a 6-hold Galleon |
| Fluyt, seventeenth-century bulk merchant | 4 / 6 / 3, deep | Merchantman approximates the role. v006 Merchantman has 5 moves and 4 holds |
| Sloop, coastal dispatch, fishery and short freight | 6 / 2 / 3, shallow | No dedicated small coastal freighter in classic or v006 |
| Schooner, particularly eighteenth-century coastal trade | 6 / 3 / 4, shallow | New niche. Make it the coastal alternative to a Merchantman |
| Brig or brigantine, medium freight or armed service | 5 / 4 / 7, medium | Can reuse the Merchantman economy role. A brig-rigged sloop also shows that rig and naval rating can overlap |
| Frigate, cruising warship | 6 / 3 / 16, medium | Classic frigate has 4 holds. Park as a combat design |
| Ship of the line, late fleet combat | 4 / 2 / 24, deep | Classic Man-o-War fills this space. Park |
| Shallop, bateaux or flatboat, inshore and inland use | 3 / 1 / 1, river and lake access | Classic has wagon trains for overland hauling. v006 supports ships and abstract overland wagon routes |

Start with **two new hulls**. Add a shallow-draft sloop and an inland bateau. Keep existing ships for ocean traffic. Later add one heavy merchant if bulk trade needs its own specialist. Treat privateering as a commission or job for suitable armed hulls. See [Royal Museums Greenwich ship collections](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections) and its [1779 brig-rigged sloop](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-66501).

## Military units and formations

These are future strategy-game abstractions. Attack and defense are relative values. The columns make no claim about historical combat probabilities.

| Unit | Moves / attack / defense | Role and classic comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Militia company | 1 / 2 / 3 | Local defense and low upkeep. Classic soldier is the nearest analogue |
| Regular infantry company | 1 / 4 / 4 | Reliable formed troops. Classic Soldier and Veteran Soldier span part of this role |
| Light infantry or ranger company | 2 / 3 / 2 | Scouting and rough terrain. Classic scout covers reconnaissance |
| Mounted dragoon detachment | 2 / 3 / 3 | Fast road movement, dismounted defense. Classic dragoon already gives a mounted role |
| Field artillery battery | 1 / 5 / 2 | Supports formed troops. Classic combines field and siege artillery and tracks damage |
| Siege train | 1 / 6 / 1 | Slow siege support, expensive logistics |
| Garrison artillery | 0 / 2 / 6 | Settlement and harbor defense, tied to a fort |

An early-period garrison could use mixed pike and shot as flavor. Later units can use line, march column, or loose order. Give line a clear-terrain combat bonus, column a movement benefit on roads, and loose order a reconnaissance or rough-ground benefit. A battery or train should need a supply slot. When rivalry work resumes, start with **three formations and two new roles**, militia and light infantry. v006 currently equips soldiers and dragoons and builds forts. Combat resolution is parked. See the National Park Service on [march columns and light infantry](https://www.nps.gov/articles/the-embattled-british-column-survival-against-the-odds-on-the-battle-road.htm) and [field artillery](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/the-hancock-cannon.htm).

## Resources and refining

| Region and resource cluster | Common processing and use | Game comparison and candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Atlantic fisheries, especially cod | Dry or salt fish for storage, provisions and trade | Classic fish become Food. v006 fishing yields Food. Add Salt and Preserved Fish |
| Northeast forests and fisheries ports | Saw lumber, build and repair hulls, produce barrels, rigging and naval stores | Classic Lumber goes to Hammers, shipbuilding uses projects. v006 has the same abstraction. Add one ship-supply recipe |
| Chesapeake tobacco and mixed grain/livestock | Cure leaf for export, mill wheat, raise animals and preserve meat | Classic tobacco to cigars and Food combine several trades. Add Grain and Provisions |
| Southern Atlantic coastal rice and indigo | Mill rice and process indigo dye | Both lack dedicated goods. Add one regional crop first, rice for provisions or indigo for textile exports |
| Caribbean cane and molasses | Mill and boil cane to sugar and molasses, distill molasses into rum | Classic and v006 compress cane and molasses into Sugar to Rum. Keep the one-step rule until there is a use for molasses |
| Andean silver and regional metals | Extract ore and refine metal. Potosí was a major silver center | Classic silver trades directly and generic Ore becomes Tools. v006 follows that shorthand. Add refining only if a dedicated mining scenario needs it |
| Local clay, sand, limestone and timber | Brick, lime, glass, masonry and carpentry | Most building materials fold into Hammers and Tools. Add one masonry material if building choices need it |
| Furs, hides, cattle and sheep | Cure hides, tan leather, make shoes and harness, spin wool | Classic Furs become Coats. Keep that chain and treat leather as a possible later supply input |
| Dye, cacao, coffee, cotton | Regional cash crops, dyestuffs and fibers, with very different time and place ranges | Cotton is already playable. Consider a single regional export slot before adding several parallel crop chains |

Limit the first additions to **three resources and two finished goods**. Grain, Salt, and a regional cash crop can feed Provisions and Preserved Fish or trade directly. Colonial exports were sustained by coercive systems and enslaved labor, especially in plantation districts. These suggestions describe commodities and geography, and the playable campaign models abstract settlement labor. See the [National Park Service Atlantic trade study](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/the-atlantic-empire-of-peter-faneuil.htm), the [Charlestown exhibit](https://www.nps.gov/fosu/learn/historyculture/upload/FOSU-VEC-exhibit-text.pdf), and [UNESCO on Potosí](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/420).

## Professions and industries

Classic has specialists for farming, fishing, trapping, timber, mining, cash crops and manufacturing, plus schooling and immigration. v006 assigns specialists by job and schools train a selected resident over three turns. It groups many local trades into a few workshop recipes.

| Profession cluster | Industries | Practical change |
|---|---|---|
| Farmer, herder, miller, baker and brewer | Mixed farms, livestock, gristmills and bakeries | Add Miller or Provisioner as one linked specialty |
| Fisher, fish curer and salt maker | Fisheries and preservation | Add Curer as the second new specialist if preserved fish ships well |
| Sawyer, carpenter, cooper, shipwright, rope maker and sailmaker | Lumber, barrel making and ship repair/construction | Keep Carpenter. Add Shipwright as a shipyard specialty before adding support trades |
| Miner, smelter, founder and smith | Mining, ironworks and toolmaking | Keep Miner and Blacksmith. Reserve a separate Smelter for a richer mining campaign |
| Spinner, weaver, dyer, tailor, tanner, shoemaker | Cloth, garment and leather trades | Keep Weaver as the visible specialist. Let input supply drive regional differences |
| Potter, brickmaker, mason and lime burner | Building materials and household wares | Add a single Mason role if masonry is introduced |
| Merchant, clerk, sailor, teamster and innkeeper | Warehousing, customs, inland transport and services | Represent these through port services and throughput |
| Teacher, preacher, printer and physician | Education, worship, printing and care | Keep Teacher and Preacher mechanics. Consider a later public-services slot |

Start with **two or three specialties and two workplaces**. Gristmill or smokehouse gives daily needs an economic home. A Shipwright gives the port a distinct identity. See [Colonial Williamsburg’s trades research and demonstrations](https://podcast.history.org/2013/06/03/creating-an-authentic-past/) and its [cooper account](https://podcast.history.org/2012/01/02/meet-the-cooper/).

## What settlements make and consume

Food, timber and tools are the main settlement inputs in the classic model. Muskets and horses equip people, tools and hammers construct buildings, and trade goods such as rum, cloth, cigars and coats are primarily valuable in trade. v006 uses food for people and breeding, tools for projects and equipment, and goods for European sales.

- **Everyday staples.** Grain, meal, bread, fish, meat, legumes and beer or cider. Use Food for immediate consumption and Provisions for voyage stores.
- **Household supplies.** Cloth, garments, shoes, soap, candles, pottery and fuel. Pick Cloth as a single optional household demand with a small recruitment benefit.
- **Construction and transport.** Boards, brick, nails, barrels, cordage and canvas. Pick one ship-supply category rather than introducing each item as a trade good.
- **Port and industrial imports.** Salt, iron goods, finished textiles, paper, glass and selected luxuries. Let ports differ by prices and availability.
- **Regional trade.** Grain and preserved fish supply ports, cane and rum support tropical export routes, tobacco and dyes bring cash, and processed metals can serve toolmaking. Different ports should offer different reasons to trade.

Start with **two recurring demands**. Provision ocean voyages and let cloth modestly improve immigration when stocked. Keep existing general Food for turn consumption. See the [Atlantic trade study](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/the-atlantic-empire-of-peter-faneuil.htm) and [Library of Congress guide to colonial commerce records](https://guides.loc.gov/colonial-america-business-research/statistics).

## Geography and terrain

| Feature | Historical relevance | Game comparison and proposed use |
|---|---|---|
| Estuary, sheltered bay, tidal river and natural harbor | Access to inland freight, sheltered anchorage and urban ports | Classic distinguishes coast, river and ocean. v006 makes coastal access important. Add a harbor tag for shipping capacity |
| Barrier islands, shoals, reefs and banks | Shelter, fishing and draft restrictions | Classic has a Fishery resource and tile-based coast art. Add one shallow-water tag |
| Floodplain, delta, tidal marsh and swamp | Different soils, water access, rice potential and transport limits | Classic marsh/swamp are base terrain. Add floodplain as an overlay that affects food or rice |
| Piedmont and fall line | Rolling uplands and limits to navigation, with mill sites at gradients | Classic hills and rivers approximate it. Add rapids/fall line as a river break and mill bonus |
| Appalachian and Andean ridges, mountain passes | Route constraints and distinct mineral districts | Classic hills/mountains and deposits. Add passes as lower movement-cost points |
| Great Lakes, major river systems and portages | Inland transport corridors with transfer points | Classic water and roads cover broad shapes. v006 permits water movement but has no inland transport rules. Add a connected navigable river network later |
| Temperate mixed forest, boreal forest, prairie, grassland, desert, arctic and tropical forests | Distinct production and movement conditions | Classic has multiple base terrains plus forest cover. v006 uses those base yields and forest state. Preserve compositing |
| Caribbean islands and archipelagos | Sea links dominate travel. Islands can specialize narrowly | Classic and v006 can render islands. Give small ports useful specialist production |

Start with **three overlays**. Add sheltered harbor, shallow water, and navigable river with fall-line breaks. A harbor tag can change shipping and repair without multiplying tile art. See the [USGS physiographic overview](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/70039236/report.pdf) and [Piedmont and Blue Ridge study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20221050/full).

## A first expansion worth testing

Use one coastal colony, one river settlement, and one European port. Try the sloop and bateau, add grain and salt to the existing trade, then see whether preserved food and harbor access create useful choices. Keep the larger list as material for regional scenarios. The bundled manual supplies the classic roster and the historical sources above supply examples. v006 implements the current peaceful campaign systems. These historical additions are proposals.

# AI design after v016

## Construction and exploration

Rivals currently choose Dock, Warehouse, Warehouse Expansion, Stockade and Fort using local demand, and budget for tools. To use the rest of the catalog they need estimates of input supply, labor, unmet demand, and freight capacity. Add one chain first: ore-producing site, blacksmith, Tools stock, and delivery to a funded construction project. Score the first building and its downstream benefit together. Protect food and freight budgets and record what prevented each viable project.

European rivals currently select a coast using global terrain, then sail to a known entry. Give each faction its own remembered map. The first ship can follow the coast toward unexplored headlands, recording rivers, anchorages, hazards, and contact. Once supplies fall or the hull is damaged, return to a known harbor. Send a land scout from a safe landing to survey nearby river valleys, then compare only surveyed settlement sites. Share discoveries with that faction's other units.

## Defenders, patrols and expeditions

Use three assignments with distinct priorities. A garrison stays near a settlement and protects minimum coverage. A patrol follows short routes between ports, roads, and border crossings and returns when supplies run low. A task force assembles from surplus units for a single objective, such as relieving a threatened colony, escorting freight, or contesting a crossing. Each unit should have one assignment, a rally point, and a fallback destination.

Use a faction-pair relationship and a local incident ledger. Rivalry alone should produce scouting, escorts and reinforced posts. An attack requires a concrete objective, sufficient local strength, supply, and an acceptable diplomatic cost. Repeated seizures, raids, or encroachment can change that calculation. European and Indigenous factions should both be able to negotiate, disengage, or reinforce instead. The current European model can harass nearby player ships during war. It has no field armies or rival-versus-rival campaign planner.

## Indigenous movement and settlement life

The current model resolves expansion and border conflict from settlement state. It has no persistent, visible warrior bands. Start with short patrols on familiar paths and a reserve near the home settlement. Let escorts accompany trade or seasonal parties, investigate incursions, warn neighboring communities, and return home. A war party needs a specific grievance and objective, allied consent where applicable, provisions, and a reason to return. Keep resistance to encroachment and opportunistic fighting as separate decisions in the ledger.

Keep most population at home producing food and goods. Model seasonal resource trips or relocation as household parties with a destination, supplies, and return or resettlement plan. A patrol should not take the workers a settlement needs for subsistence. Communities should vary their range and response according to terrain, local ties, and circumstances.

## Test fixtures

Run one coastal survey with hidden shores, one tools supply chain under freight pressure, one attack that is called off when a garrison arrives, and one border escalation that de-escalates after diplomacy. Log discovered tiles, time to first viable site, garrison coverage, supplies, goal changes, and the specific incident behind every attack. These fixtures should supplement the eight 200-turn campaign slots.
